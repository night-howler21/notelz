## NOTELZ

## Website Vision & Product Design Brief

No one tells you what to study at the last minute — so we will.

## THE IDEA

A sophisticated digital notebook for students: notes, last-minute revision, tutors and one-to-one learning in one calm, organised space.

## THE ANCHOR

Green is the primary visual language — inspired by Mercury as a symbol of knowledge, wisdom, communication and learning.

## Design principle

Notelz should feel like opening a beautifully maintained notebook rather than entering another loud educational platform. The interface is functional first, but its visual language should make studying feel personal, calm and slightly ceremonial.

## THE FEEL

Pastel stationery, ruled paper, handwriting, quiet academic elegance, botanical details and a sense of personal ownership.

## THE PROMISE

Less searching. Less panic. More clarity about exactly what to study and how to study it.


## 01 — Visual Direction

The website should look like a sophisticated notebook cover translated into a digital interface. It should not look childish, overly corporate, or aggressively 'Gen-Z'. The aesthetic is soft, intelligent and editorial.

## Visual vocabulary

- Paper: warm off-white backgrounds, subtle paper grain and faint ruled lines.

- Typography: refined serif or humanist display type for headings, paired with a highly readable sans-serif for interface text; handwriting reserved for notes, annotations and selected brand moments.

- Illustration: delicate botanical sketches, books, pens, stars, Mercury/planet motifs and small academic doodles.

- Shapes: rounded but restrained cards, paper tabs, binder-ring details, underlines and soft shadows.

- Motion: gentle page transitions, hover previews that feel like lifting a sticky note, and subtle micro-interactions rather than flashy animation.

## 03 — Notes: The Core Product

The notes section should be the strongest expression of the Notelz identity. It should feel like a living digital notebook, not a PDF viewer pasted into a website.

## Subject → Topic → Note

- Subject: e.g. Constitutional Law.

- Topic: e.g. Doctrine of Eclipse.

- Note: the complete study material, presented on a continuous ruled-paper canvas.

- Navigation: the topic list remains anchored on the left so students can move between topics without leaving the reader.

- Account panel: library, saved files, custom lists and personal study organisation remain accessible on the right or in a collapsible panel.

## Hover preview

When a student hovers over a topic such as Doctrine of Eclipse, a small paper-like preview should appear beside the topic. It can contain a one- or two-line definition, key case, or exam cue. The preview should feel like a tiny sticky note being revealed.

## Click → stay inside the notebook

Clicking the topic opens the complete note in the central canvas. The page should scroll naturally like a notebook. The student should not be bounced between separate browser pages or forced to download a document simply to read it.

## Account experience

- A clean personal dashboard showing saved notes, saved PDFs, custom lists and recently opened topics.

- Users can create an account to unlock private annotations and persistent saving.

- The account should never feel like an administrative portal; it should retain the notebook aesthetic.

- Students should be able to return to exactly where they left off.

## Aesthetic interaction details

- Hovering over a topic: tiny paper preview / sticky note.

- Saving: subtle bookmark animation.

- Opening a note: soft page transition rather than a hard route change.

- Adding a doodle: pen cursor and familiar stationery controls.

- Switching topics: preserve reading position and keep the left topic index stable.

- Mobile: transform the sidebars into drawer panels while preserving the ruled-paper reading surface.


## 05 — Supporting Tabs

## ABOUT

Brand story, educational philosophy, who creates the notes, editorial standards and the meaning behind the Notelz identity.

## BLOGS

Long-form academic explainers, study strategy, legal developments, exam guidance and carefully curated educational commentary.

## SERVICES

A central catalogue for 1–1 sessions, tutors, subject notes and last-minute revision products.

## NOTIFICATIONS

Quiet, useful updates: saved tutor responses, session reminders, newly released notes, list activity and important account

notifications.

## NOTES

The primary knowledge library. Subjects, topics, revision collections and personalised note-reading experience.

## Information architecture

Home → Service selection → Subject / Tutor / Session → Note or session experience → Save → Personal Library

The architecture should minimise dead ends. Wherever a student discovers useful material, there should be an obvious way to save it, continue reading, add it to a list, or return to the broader subject.


## 06 — Product & Design Handoff

The first design sprint should answer these questions:

- What is the final Notelz logo and symbol?

- Which green becomes the official primary brand colour?

- Which 4–6 supporting pastels form the permanent design system?

- Which font combination gives us the right balance between sophistication and handwriting?

- How much handwriting is appropriate before readability suffers?

- What exactly is free, and what requires an account or payment?

- Which annotation features are included in V1?

- Will PDFs be uploaded by the Notelz team, by verified contributors, or by users?

- What moderation / quality-control process applies to educational content?

- What is the mobile-first behaviour of the notebook reader?
